# AIM and Feline Kidney Disease (FeliAIM)

content drafting only. It is not pre-approved messaging.

## What AIM is

AIM (Apoptosis Inhibitor of Macrophage), also known as CD5L, is a blood
protein studied by Prof. Toru Miyazaki's research group (University of
Tokyo / AIM医学研究所). In mice, AIM dissociates from IgM pentamers during
acute kidney injury, moves into the urine, binds necrotic debris blocking
the proximal tubules, and interacts with KIM-1 (kidney injury molecule-1)
so that surviving tubular cells can clear the debris — supporting recovery
from acute kidney injury.
Source: Nature Medicine study on AIM and acute kidney injury (PMID 26726878).

## Why cats are thought to be vulnerable

In cats, AIM is bound to IgM unusually tightly and does not dissociate
efficiently even when the kidney is injured. Because AIM cannot detach and
reach the tubules, debris is not cleared as effectively, which is
hypothesized to contribute to the high prevalence of chronic kidney
disease (CKD) in cats. This is one contributing mechanism among several —
age, dehydration, hypertension, infection, congenital disease, and urinary
tract disorders also contribute to feline CKD, and AIM dysfunction should
not be presented as the sole cause.
Source: AIM医学研究所 outline page (iamaim.jp/en/outline).

## FeliAIM (recombinant AIM therapy)

- FeliAIM is a recombinant AIM protein (rAIM) administered by injection —
  not a gene therapy and not a food additive. It supplements the cat's own
  poorly-mobilized AIM.
- A preliminary clinical study (published early 2026 in *The Veterinary
  Journal*) treated cats with advanced CKD (serum creatinine 2.9–5.0 mg/dL,
  consistent with IRIS Stage 3b): 6 cats received mouse rAIM, 5 received
  feline rAIM (11 treated total), versus 15 untreated controls, monitored
  over 360 days.
  - 360-day cumulative survival: approximately 80–83% in the rAIM-treated
    groups vs. approximately 20% in untreated controls.
  - rAIM also stabilized kidney biomarkers (creatinine, indoxyl sulfate,
    SDMA), reduced systemic inflammatory markers, and restored serum
    sphingomyelin levels in the treated groups.
  - This was a small, non-blinded preliminary study. Larger trials are
    needed to establish safety, effective dose, target disease stage, and
    treatment duration before any efficacy claim is made publicly.
- A nationwide clinical trial toward regulatory approval began around
  May–June 2025 across 26 veterinary hospitals in Japan.
- The product's official name, FeliAIM (フェリエイム), was finalized in
  early 2026.
- On 2026-04-24, IAM CAT Inc. filed a marketing authorization application
  for FeliAIM as an animal drug with Japan's Ministry of Agriculture,
  Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF).

## Current regulatory status (verify before each use — this changes)

As of this writing, FeliAIM is **filed for approval, not yet approved and
not yet commercially available**. Do not state or imply that it is on sale,
prescribable, or in stock at any Puppily location without re-verifying the
approval status on the date of publication.

## What FeliAIM is not

- It is not proven to reverse already-lost nephrons or established renal
  fibrosis. Present it as a candidate that may slow progression, reduce
  uremic toxin burden, and extend survival — not as a cure or a way to
  restore normal kidney tissue.
- It is not the same thing as "AIM" or "AIM30"-branded foods. AIM is a
  protein and is digested if eaten; injectable rAIM cannot be delivered
  through food. Marketed foods claim only to support the cat's own AIM
  activation (e.g. helping endogenous AIM detach from IgM), which is a
  different mechanism from injecting rAIM. Do not imply that feeding such a
  food is equivalent to receiving FeliAIM treatment, and do not claim CKD
  is treated by diet alone.

## Points to re-verify before any approval-related announcement

- Whether MAFF has approved FeliAIM, and the approval/launch date.
- The approved IRIS stage(s) / indication.
- Dosing interval and treatment duration in the approved label.
- Conditions/contraindications for cats with concurrent renal anemia,
  hyperphosphatemia, or hypertension.
- Reported adverse events, including immune reactions or anti-drug
  antibodies.
- How FeliAIM is positioned alongside existing standard care (renal diet,
  phosphorus control, hydration, blood pressure and proteinuria
  management, potassium correction, antiemetics, anemia treatment) — do
  not imply standard care becomes unnecessary.
